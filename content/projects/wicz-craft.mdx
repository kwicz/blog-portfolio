Wicz Craft is a butter company that doesn't exist. Twelve flavors of cultured butter, made in Portland, wrapped in foil, each with its own pair of colors. I made it up so I'd have a real-feeling store to build on Shopify, end to end, from a written design guide.

The tricky part was the box builder. Shoppers pick a box (four, six, eight, or one 8 oz with a few 2 oz cubes) and fill it from the flavor grid. Every box has one flat price, whatever goes in. That sounds simple until you notice a box can cost more than the same butters bought one at a time, which rules out Shopify's usual bundle merge. So the box goes into the cart as a single line, and a Cart Transform function expands it into the butters inside. The price comes from the box you bought, never from the cart properties, and a box that doesn't follow the rules just stays a box.

Each flavor carries its colors in metafields, so the product page re-themes itself: caesar is pistachio and tomato, chili crisp is oxblood and tangerine. Liquid checks the contrast and swaps colors when a pair fails. An audit script then walks each page checking the guide's rules: lowercase, two corner radii, one gradient, light inks only at display sizes.

The whole catalog lives in one file. Two seed scripts load the products, metafields, collections and 100-odd photos through the Admin API, so a fresh store is one command away. The side drawer cart, the upsell rows and the photo strips came later, piled on the same way.
