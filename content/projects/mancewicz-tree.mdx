I found out a relative of mine worked at the Pullman factory and started trying to trace it back. That turned into a pretty deep rabbit hole about where the family actually came from in Poland, and at some point a spreadsheet stopped being enough.

The Mancewicz Tree covers 98 people across three immigrant lines, Polish, Prussian, and Irish, that ended up in Chicago. The content is files, not form fields: people, places, facts, and sources live as JSON and markdown in the repo, versioned in git, and every deploy syncs them into Postgres. Accounts, uploaded photos, and family anecdotes survive the sync untouched; derived data like relationships gets rebuilt. That means the research has a review trail, a diff history, and no way to silently drift from what the records say.

The tree itself is a custom chart you can pan around, with hourglass, ancestor, and descendant views and hover-to-trace lineage paths. Person pages read like a case file: a narrative summary, a mini-tree, dated facts each tied to a source, the name variants records actually used (census takers were not careful spellers), and the places they lived. A Leaflet map plots all 55 places the records name, from a Polish village no modern map shows to specific Chicago street addresses.

The site is public because dead ancestors belong to everyone descended from them, but living relatives render as unnamed placeholders until family members sign in. Relatives can contribute anecdotes typed or recorded, audio gets transcribed automatically, and everything publishes immediately.
